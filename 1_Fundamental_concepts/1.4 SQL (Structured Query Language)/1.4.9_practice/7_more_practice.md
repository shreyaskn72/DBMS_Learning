Here are several **SQL interview-style questions** similar in complexity to the "second highest salary" problem. These test your understanding of **subqueries, aggregation, sorting, filtering, and joins** — all important for SQL interviews.

---

### 🔹 **Ranking and Aggregation**

1. **Third Highest Salary**:
   Find the third highest distinct salary from the `employee` table.

2. **Nth Highest Salary**:
   Write a query to find the N-th highest salary (pass N as a parameter).

3. **Employees with Top 3 Salaries**:
   Get the full details of employees whose salaries are among the top 3 distinct salaries.

---

### 🔹 **Joins and Grouping**

4. **Department with Highest Average Salary**:
   Find the department name(s) that has the highest average employee salary.

5. **Employees Without a Manager**:
   Assuming the employee table has a `manager_id` column, list all employees who do not have a manager.

6. **Employees Who Earn More Than Their Manager**:
   Find employees who earn more than their own manager.

---

### 🔹 **Subqueries and Filtering**

7. **Employees with Salary Above Department Average**:
   List employees who earn more than the average salary of their respective departments.

8. **Employees Who Have the Same Salary as Someone Else**:
   Find employees who share the same salary with at least one other employee.

---

### 🔹 **Date and Time**

9. **Most Recently Joined Employee in Each Department**:
   For each department, find the most recently hired employee.

10. **Count of Employees Hired Each Year**:
    Group the employees by hiring year and show the count per year.
